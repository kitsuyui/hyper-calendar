# Russia's public holidays and the transfers of days off

Backs the `RUSSIA` table (`RU`) in `hc-holiday`, and the Moscow Exchange
(`MISX`), which does not include it and follows only part of it.

## What it is

Russia's public holidays rest on one article of statute and one decree a
year. Article 112 of the Трудовой кодекс Российской Федерации (Labour
Code, Federal Law 197-ФЗ of 30 December 2001) lists the нерабочие
праздничные дни, the non-working holidays: 1 to 6 and 8 January, the
Новогодние каникулы; 7 January, Рождество Христово; 23 February, День
защитника Отечества; 8 March, Международный женский день; 1 May,
Праздник Весны и Труда; 9 May, День Победы; 12 June, День России; and
4 November, День народного единства [ru-labour-code-art112]. The list
took that shape in two amendments: Federal Law 201-ФЗ of 29 December
2004 made the New Year holidays 1 to 5 January, added Unity Day on
4 November from 2005 and dropped 2 May and 7 November; Federal Law
35-ФЗ of 23 April 2012 added 6 and 8 January, so that the holidays run
from 1 to 8 January [ru-labour-code-art112].

The article then says what happens when a holiday falls on a weekend.
Its second part: "При совпадении выходного и нерабочего праздничного дней
выходной день переносится на следующий после праздничного рабочий день"
— the day off is carried over to the first working day after the
holiday — "за исключением выходных дней, совпадающих с нерабочими
праздничными днями, указанными в абзацах втором и третьем части первой",
except for the January days. For those, "Правительство Российской
Федерации переносит два выходных дня из числа выходных дней, совпадающих
с нерабочими праздничными днями, указанными в абзацах втором и третьем
части первой настоящей статьи, на другие дни в очередном календарном
году": the Government moves two of the January weekend days to other
days of the coming year. Its fifth part lets days off be moved "в целях
рационального использования работниками выходных и нерабочих праздничных
дней" by federal law or by a Government act, which must be published no
later than a month before the year begins, or two months before a day
it moves [ru-labour-code-art112].

**The annual decree.** Every year, then, the Government issues a
постановление «О переносе выходных дней в NNNN году», between July and
October of the year before. It is one sentence: "Перенести в NNNN году
следующие выходные дни:" and a list of "с субботы 6 января на пятницу
10 мая" — from this day to that. Two kinds of line appear. The two
January weekend days the article makes it move go to weekdays beside
other holidays, typically the days between 1 and 9 May, or 31 December.
And a Saturday that sits between a holiday and a weekend is often swapped
for the working day on the other side — "с субботы 27 апреля на
понедельник 29 апреля" — so that the Saturday is worked and the Monday is
off. A holiday outside January that falls on a weekend is not mentioned
by the decree unless it moves that day too; the article's carry-over
handles it. The result is a year in which some Mondays and Fridays are
days off and some Saturdays are working days, and a caller cannot know
which from the statute; ConsultantPlus, Garant and every payroll office
publish a производственный календарь, a production calendar, for the
year that folds the article and the decree together
[ru-production-calendars].

than undoing it [ru-transfer-decrees].

## How it works

**From the decree to the table.** `RU_TRANSFERS` holds one row per line of
each decree: the year, the month and day moved from, the month and day
moved to. From those rows three functions derive the year's entries, each
a `Rule::Tabulated` bounded to 2013–2027 so that a year outside is a gap:

- `ru_transferred(year)`: every "to" day, as a public holiday named "Day
  off transferred by the Government" (Перенесённый выходной день).
- `ru_worked(year)`: every "from" day that is a Saturday or Sunday and not
  itself a holiday of Article 112, as a `Kind::Workday` entry named
  "Working day, a day off transferred by the Government" (Рабочий день).
  A January holiday on the weekend keeps its holiday when its weekend is
  moved — 6 January 2024 was a Saturday and stayed a day off — so it is
  not a working day; a Saturday 27 April is.
- `ru_carried_over(year)`: the article's own rule, computed rather than
  tabulated because the decree can reach into it. For each holiday outside
  January that falls on a weekend and that the decree does not move, the
  function walks forward to the first day that is not a weekend day (or
  is a weekend day the decree works), not a holiday, not a transferred day
  and not already carried over, and makes that a public holiday named "Day
  off carried over from a holiday on the weekend" — unless the decree
  moves that day, as in 2014. The January days are excluded as the article
  excludes them.

The holidays themselves are fixed rules, `fixed_public` since Russia has
no general substitution law: the article's carry-over is a rule about the
weekend day, not the holiday, and the holiday keeps its date. A working
day is an entry, per [ADR 0009](../adr/0009-a-working-day-is-an-entry.md),
so `HolidayCalendar::is_business_day` is true on a worked Saturday and
`is_designated_workday` says so.

**The exchange.** The Moscow Exchange does not follow the decree. Its
announcements for 2023 to 2026 close the market on the holiday dates
themselves — 1, 2 and 7 January, 23 February, 8 March, 1 and 9 May,
12 June, 4 November — and, from 2024, on 31 December, a day the decree
transfers there each year; and they trade on the other New Year days, on
the transferred and carried-over days off, and "в обычном режиме" on the
Saturdays the decree works. `MOSCOW_EXCHANGE` therefore includes nothing:
its closures are tabulated from its announcements, and its working
Saturdays are its own `Kind::Workday` entries, since an included table's
working days would not be lent in any case. In 2026 four holidays have
only the weekend-day session, whose trades belong to the next trading day;
they are closures named for it. A year outside 2023–2026 is a gap
[moex-holiday-schedules].

**Worked example: 2024.** Decree № 1314 of 10 August 2023, published in
Российская газета on 20 August 2023 and on the legal information portal
on 18 August, says [ru-transfer-decree-2024]:

> Перенести в 2024 году следующие выходные дни: с субботы 6 января на
> пятницу 10 мая; с воскресенья 7 января на вторник 31 декабря; с субботы
> 27 апреля на понедельник 29 апреля; с субботы 2 ноября на вторник
> 30 апреля; с субботы 28 декабря на понедельник 30 декабря.

In the table those are five rows keyed 2024: `(2024, 1, 6, 5, 10)`,
`(2024, 1, 7, 12, 31)`, `(2024, 4, 27, 4, 29)`, `(2024, 11, 2, 4, 30)`
and `(2024, 12, 28, 12, 30)`. `ru_transferred(2024)` is 29 and 30 April,
10 May, 30 and 31 December. `ru_worked(2024)` is 27 April, 2 November and
28 December: 6 and 7 January are weekend days but holidays, so they drop
out. `ru_carried_over(2024)` is empty — 23 February and 8 March are
Fridays, 1 May, 9 May and 12 June are weekdays, and 4 November is a
Monday. The year's days off on weekdays are therefore 1 to 5 and
8 January, 23 February, 8 March, 29 and 30 April, 1, 9 and 10 May,
12 June, 4 November, 30 and 31 December — seventeen — and the weekend
days worked are three, which is what the production calendar prints
[ru-production-calendars].

Ask a calendar built for `RUSSIA` and 2024 about Saturday 27 April:
`is_weekend` is true, `is_designated_workday` is true, `is_holiday` is
false and `is_business_day` is true; `add_business_days` from Friday
26 April by one lands on it, and by two on Thursday 2 May, since Monday
29, Tuesday 30 April and Wednesday 1 May are all days off. Monday
29 April `is_holiday` with the name "Day off transferred by the
Government". Saturday 6 January is a holiday and not a business day; the
transfer moved its weekend, not its holiday. Built for `MOSCOW_EXCHANGE`
instead, 27 April is a trading day too, from the exchange's own
`Working day, a working Saturday` entry; 29 and 30 April are trading days,
which Russia's table says are days off; 1 May is closed; and 31 December
is closed while 30 December trades.

## What is carried

- **The holidays of Article 112**, with the years the list changed: 1 and
  2 January (the second from 1993), 3 to 5 January from 2005, 6 and
  8 January from 2013, 7 January from 1991, 23 February from 2002, 8 March,
  1 May, 2 May to 2004, 9 May, 12 June from 1992, 7 November 1996–2004,
  4 November from 2005. The 2004 and 2012 changes are in the article's
  own history; the earlier years — 1991, 1992, 1993, 1996 and 2002 — come
  from the code's secondary source and no statute read here.
- **The transfer decrees for 2013 to 2027**, fifteen decrees, 46 rows:
  33 weekend days that are holidays and stay so (the January days, and
  23 February 2019 and 2025 and 8 March 2025), eleven Saturdays worked
  (2016, three in 2018, 2021, 2022, three in 2024, 2025 and 2027), and
  the two Mondays of 2013 and 2014; and Article 112's carry-over computed
  against them.
- **The gap rule.** A year without a decree here — 2012 and earlier, 2028
  and later — is a gap: `is_complete` is false and `gaps` names the three
  tabulated rules. The decree for 2028 is due by the end of November 2027.
- **Not carried, and why:**
  - The decrees before 2013, and the carry-over for those years, which
    the same function would compute wrongly without them; a year before
    2013 is a gap rather than a half answer.
  - The President's нерабочие дни of 2020 and 2021 — the paid non-working
    days of the pandemic and of May and November 2021, declared by decree
    of the President and not days off under Article 112. The production
    calendars do not count them, and the table follows the calendars. The
    decrees were not read for this document.
  - The предпраздничный день, the working day before a holiday shortened
    by an hour under Article 95, which is a working day.
  - Regional holidays that the republics set under Article 112's
    reference to the Federal Law on freedom of conscience — Kurban Bayram
    in Tatarstan and Bashkortostan, for instance — which would need
    subdivisions the table does not have.
  - The Labour Code's predecessor, the КЗоТ of the RSFSR, whose article
    65 governed to 2002; the years before are what the secondary source
    says, above.

## Accuracy

`russia_matches_its_production_calendar_from_2013_to_2027` in
`crates/hc-holiday/tests/countries.rs` walks every day of every year from
2013 to 2027 and compares the weekdays on which `is_business_day` is
false, and the weekend days on which it is true, with ConsultantPlus's
production calendar for the year, transcribed into `RU_PRODUCTION_CALENDARS`
in the test. All fifteen years agree in both lists. That is the check on
the decrees as carried and on the carry-over as computed, together.
`russia_carries_a_weekend_holiday_over_unless_the_decree_moves_it` pins
the three shapes: a plain carry-over (Sunday 8 March 2020 to Monday the
9th), a forestalled one (Sunday 23 February 2025 to 8 May, not the 24th),
a moved one (Monday 24 February 2014), a worked Saturday (27 April 2024)
and a January holiday whose weekend was moved (6 January 2024).
`russia_grew_its_new_year_holidays_and_carries_no_transfers` pins the
list and its years.

For the exchange,
`moscow_closes_on_the_days_its_announcements_list_from_2023_to_2026` and
`moscow_trades_on_russias_other_days_off_and_its_working_saturdays` in
`crates/hc-holiday/tests/exchanges.rs` compare the exchange's closures
with its announcements and show the fourteen days off on which it trades
and the four Saturdays on which both it and the country work.

Known points a reader may stumble on:

- The decrees for 2013 to 2023 were not re-read for this document; their
  numbers and dates are as the table's comments give them, from
  ConsultantPlus and Garant as the table cites, and their content is
  confirmed by the production calendars, which cite them.
- Only 2024 (decree and publication), 2025, 2026 and 2027 were read for
  this document, 2025 and 2026 through the production calendar's
  transcription of the decree's lines.
- ConsultantPlus's calendar is itself a reading of the article and the
  decree, not a primary source; it is what employers use, and a
  disagreement between it and the engine would be a bug in one of them.
- The table's own `sources` string dates the 2012 amendment "35-ФЗ of
  2012" without a day; it is 23 April 2012.

## Code

`crates/hc-holiday/src/countries/europe.rs`: the rows in `RU_TRANSFERS`
with `RU_TRANSFERS_FIRST` and `RU_TRANSFERS_LAST`, the holiday list
`RU_OUTSIDE_JANUARY`, the derivations `ru_transfers`, `ru_transferred`,
`ru_worked` and `ru_carried_over`, the rules in `RU_RULES` and the table
`RUSSIA`. The engine's part is `Rule::Tabulated`, `Kind::Workday` and
`HolidayRule::workday` in `rule.rs`, and `is_designated_workday` and
`is_business_day` in `engine.rs`. The exchange is `MOSCOW_EXCHANGE` in
`exchanges.rs`, with `MISX_RULES` and `MISX_WORKING_SATURDAYS`.

Anchors, in `crates/hc-holiday/tests/countries.rs`:
`russia_grew_its_new_year_holidays_and_carries_no_transfers`,
`russia_matches_its_production_calendar_from_2013_to_2027` and
`russia_carries_a_weekend_holiday_over_unless_the_decree_moves_it`. In
`crates/hc-holiday/tests/exchanges.rs`:
`moscow_closes_on_the_days_its_announcements_list_from_2023_to_2026` and
`moscow_trades_on_russias_other_days_off_and_its_working_saturdays`.
