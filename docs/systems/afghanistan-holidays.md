# Afghanistan's holidays under the Islamic Emirate

Backs the `AFGHANISTAN` table (`AF`) in `hc-holiday`, and its calendar
system `CalendarSystem::SOLAR_HIJRI_AFGHAN`, which names the
`persian-afghan` calendar of `hc-calendars-equinox`.

## What it is

Since the Islamic Emirate took Kabul on 15 August 2021, Afghanistan's
public holidays have not been a list in a statute. Two bodies set them.
The Ministry of Information and Culture prints an official calendar for
each lunar Hijri year, which the ministries and offices work to; the first
was that of 1444 AH, from 1 Muharram, 8 Asad 1401 (30 July 2022), to
27 Saratan 1402 [rukhshana2022]. The Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs
then announces each holiday shortly before it falls, and it is those
notices that the press — the state Bakhtar agency and Anis daily among
it — and the Emirate's own site carry [alemarah2024-victory] [alemarah2024-independence]
[pajhwok2023] [anis2025] [bakhtar2026].

The calendar itself was not read. What is known of the first comes from
Rukhshana Media, which obtained a copy: the year's days off fell from
fifteen to nine; the holidays of the first and second days of the solar
year, Nowruz, were dropped, as were those of 8 Saur, the mujahedin's entry
into Kabul in 1992, of Ashura on 10 Muharram and of the Prophet's Birthday
on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal; 24 Asad, the day the Emirate took Kabul, was added
and "declared a holiday"; and the holidays of 28 Asad, the recovery of
independence in 1919, and of 26 Dalw, the Soviet withdrawal of 1989, were
kept [rukhshana2022]. The Afghanistan Analysts Network's history of the
country's national days describes the same August — 15 August, 19 August
and, from 2023, 31 August for the departure of the last United States
troops, for which it cites a France 24 report rather than a notice
[foschini2023].

## How it works

Two calendars date the days. The three civil days are solar: the official
calendar gives each lunar date "with its solar equivalent", and the
Ministry's notices name the solar day — "فردا يكشنبه ۲۸ اسد", tomorrow,
Sunday 28 Asad, in 1403 [alemarah2024-independence]. So 24 Asad, 28 Asad
and 26 Dalw are fixed dates of the Solar Hijri calendar as Afghanistan keeps
it, whose Asad and Dalw begin on the days Iran's Mordad and Bahman do,
and they drift against the Gregorian calendar with the equinox: 26 Dalw
1403 is 14 February 2025, 26 Dalw 1404 the 15th of February 2026.

The religious days are lunar, and the notice fixes both the day and how
many follow it. The Supreme Court declares the month on the sighting; the
Ministry then gives a number of *working* days. Its notice for 1447 AH
reads, in the Bakhtar agency's report: Arafah is Tuesday 9 Dhu al-Hijjah,
four working days are holidays, and the offices return on Sunday
14 Dhu al-Hijjah [bakhtar2026]. Worked through: Tuesday 26 May 2026 is
the 9th; Wednesday, Thursday and Saturday, the 10th, 11th and 13th, are the
other three working days; Friday the 12th is the weekend and is not
counted; Sunday 31 May is the 14th. So the holidays run from 9 to 13 Dhu
al-Hijjah. The notice of 1446 gave Arafah on a Friday and four working
days after it, Saturday 10 to Tuesday 13 [anis2025]. That of 1444 gave
Arafah and three days of Eid, and was then extended to Saturday the 13th,
the offices returning on Sunday the 14th [pajhwok2023]. For Eid al-Fitr
the one notice read about is that of 1445, which declared Saturday
13 April 2024 a holiday for the Eid [khaama2024]; how many days the first
notice gave was not read.

The notices also show the week. "Four working days" from a Tuesday to a
Sunday return leaves out the Friday and counts the Thursday and the
Saturday [bakhtar2026]: the weekend is Friday alone.

## What is carried

From 2023, the first year wholly under the Emirate's calendars:

- **Victory Day**, 24 Asad; **Independence Day**, 28 Asad; **Liberation
  Day**, 26 Dalw — on `persian-afghan`, exact.
- **Eid al-Fitr**, 1 Shawwal, on the tabular Hijri calendar, approximate.
  The days after it are the notice's to fix, and in no year was the full
  count read, so they are a gap in every year.
- **Arafah** and **Eid al-Adha** from 9 to 12 Dhu al-Hijjah, which every
  notice read gives, approximate; 13 Dhu al-Hijjah in 2023, 2025 and 2026,
  the years whose notices were read, and a gap in 2024 and from 2027.

Not carried: the Republic's list before 2023, with Nowruz, 8 Saur, Ashura
and the Prophet's Birthday; the calendar's commemorations that are not
days off; 31 August, which the press reports as declared in 2023 and no
notice read confirms; and the lengthening of an Eid beyond the days above.
Nothing moves off the Friday weekend, and no notice read moves a holiday
off it.

## Accuracy

The solar days are exact to the calendar: 24 and 28 Asad 1403 are
Wednesday 14 and Sunday 18 August 2024, the weekdays the Emirate's site
gives with them [alemarah2024-victory] [alemarah2024-independence]. The
calendar's own accuracy, and the four years of its civil period that turn
on whose noon decides 1 Hamal, are `persian-afghan`'s and are set out in
its module; none of them falls in the years this table covers.

The Eid days are a prediction. The tabular calendar puts 10 Dhu al-Hijjah
1446 on Saturday 7 June 2025 and 9 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447 on Tuesday 26 May
2026, which is where the notices put them [anis2025] [bakhtar2026], but
10 Dhu al-Hijjah 1444 on Thursday 29 June 2023, a day after the Eid the
1444 notice implies [pajhwok2023]. It puts 1 Shawwal 1445 on Wednesday
10 April 2024; no notice read gives that day.
