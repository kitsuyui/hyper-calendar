# East Asian folk days and almanac cycles: お盆, the sexagenary-day festivals, 恵方, 三元九運 and 손 없는 날

Backs `hc-holiday`'s `obon-july`, `obon-august`, `obon-lunar`,
`tori-no-ichi`, `hatsuuma`, `hatsuuma-lunar`, `inoko`, `inoko-november`,
`tokanya` and `tokanya-november`, and `hc-almanac`'s `lucky_direction`,
`nine_periods` and `days_without_son`. None is a registered calendar: the
festivals are tables of days, and the rest are readings laid over the
sexagenary cycle and the lunar calendars.

## What it is

- **お盆** is the Japanese festival of the ancestors' spirits. Until the
  calendar reform of 1872 it was kept around the fifteenth of the seventh
  month. The reform's notice made the Gregorian 15 July the rule, and that
  fell in the busiest weeks of the farming year, so most of Japan moved it a
  month later to 15 August. Three reckonings are kept today: 新暦 July, in
  Tokyo and some cities of Tōhoku and Hokuriku; 月遅れ August, in most of
  the country; and 旧盆 on the lunar calendar, in Okinawa and Amami
  [wikipedia-ja-obon].
- **酉の市** is the fair held on the 酉 days of November at the 鷲 and 大鳥
  shrines of the Kantō, where decorated rakes are sold for the coming year's
  trade [wikipedia-ja-tori-no-ichi].
- **初午** is the festival of the Inari shrines on the first 午 day of the
  second month, the day the god is said to have come down on Mount Inari in
  711 [wikipedia-ja-hatsuuma, inari-hatsuuma].
- **亥の子**, mainly in western Japan, and **十日夜**, mainly in eastern Japan,
  are harvest festivals of the tenth lunar month: 亥の子 on its first 亥 day
  with 亥の子餅 and children pounding the ground with a stone, 十日夜 on its
  tenth with straw bundles [wikipedia-ja-inoko, wikipedia-ja-tokanya].
- **恵方** is the direction in which the deity of the year's fortune, 歳徳神,
  stands. The New Year's shrine visit, 恵方参り, is made in that direction,
  and the 恵方巻 of 節分 is eaten facing it [kotobank-eho].
- **三元九運** is the reckoning of 玄空 feng shui: periods of twenty years,
  each ruled by one of the nine stars [chanweitang-sanyuan-jiuyun].
- **손 없는 날**, "days without *son*", are the Korean lunar days on which
  the harmful spirit *son* is abroad in no direction, and which Koreans
  choose for moving house, weddings and opening a business
  [wikipedia-ko-son-eomneun-nal].

## How it works

### お盆

| Reckoning | Days | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7月盆 | 13–16 July | Tokyo, parts of Tōhoku, Hokuriku and Shizuoka |
| 月遅れ盆 | 13–16 August | most of Japan |
| 旧盆 | 旧暦 7/13–7/15 | Okinawa and Amami |

The 迎え火, the fire that welcomes the spirits, is lit on the evening of the
13th, and the 送り火 that sends them back on the 16th, or in many places the
15th. Okinawa names the lunar days ウンケー (7/13), ナカビ (7/14) and ウークイ
(7/15, or 7/16 in some places). The 旧暦 fifteenth falls from 8 August to
7 September: in 1991–2030 it is earliest on 8 August 2006 and latest on
6 September 2025. A leap seventh month does not repeat it, so the 15th of
2006's leap month, 7 September, was not 旧盆 [wikipedia-ja-obon].

### The days of the sexagenary cycle

Every day carries one of the twelve earthly branches, in a cycle of twelve
days, so a month holds two or three days of each.

- **酉の市**: every 酉 day of Gregorian November, 一の酉, 二の酉 and, in some
  years, 三の酉. A month of 30 days holds a third 酉 exactly when the first
  falls on 1 to 6 November [wikipedia-ja-tori-no-ichi].
- **初午**: the first 午 day of February on the Gregorian calendar, with the
  second and third, 二の午 and 三の午, kept in some places; before the
  reform, and in some places still, the first 午 of the second lunar month
  [wikipedia-ja-hatsuuma].
- **亥の子**: the first 亥 day of the tenth lunar month, 亥の月
  [wikipedia-ja-inoko], or, as it is usually reckoned today, the first 亥 of
  Gregorian November [allabout-inoko-2025].
- **十日夜**: 旧暦 10/10 [wikipedia-ja-tokanya], or in many places the
  Gregorian 10 November, the lunar date moved a month [jpnculture-tokanya].

**Worked example.** 1 November 2026 is RD 739 921. The day's branch is
(RD + 14) mod 12 = 739 935 mod 12 = 3, 卯. 酉 is branch 9, six days on, so
一の酉 is 7 November, 二の酉 twelve days later on 19 November, and a third
would be 1 December, outside the month: 鷲神社 holds the fair on 7 and
19 November 2026, "二の酉まで" [otorisama-kotoshi]. The first 亥, branch 11,
is two days after 7 November: 9 November, which is also the first day of
the tenth lunar month of 2026, so both reckonings of 亥の子 fall on it that
year [koyominote-inoko].

### 恵方

The direction is the year's heavenly stem. Only the five yang stems have a
direction of their own, and each yin stem borrows its partner's
[kotobank-eho]:

| Stems | Last digit of the year | 恵方 | Between | Azimuth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 甲, 己 | 4, 9 | 甲 | 寅 and 卯 | 75° |
| 乙, 庚 | 5, 0 | 庚 | 申 and 酉 | 255° |
| 丙, 辛, 戊, 癸 | 6, 1, 8, 3 | 丙 | 巳 and 午 | 165° |
| 丁, 壬 | 7, 2 | 壬 | 亥 and 子 | 345° |

The azimuths are the centres of the stems' points among the twenty-four,
each 15° wide [wikipedia-ja-toshitokujin]. **Worked example.** 2026 ends in
6, a 丙 year (丙午), so its 恵方 is 丙, 165°, a little south of
south-south-east, as the 恵方巻 of 3 February 2026 faced [allabout-eho-2026,
jre-eho-2026].

### 三元九運

Twenty years make a period, 運, three periods an era, 元, of sixty years,
and the three eras 上元, 中元 and 下元 a cycle of 180. The current cycle
began in 1864, a 甲子 year, with 上元一運, ruled by 一白. Each period is
ruled by the star of its number: 1864–1883 一白, 1884–1903 二黒, and so on to
2024–2043, 九紫. A period begins at 立春 of its first year: 八運 ran "从2004年
立春起至2024年立春止" [chanweitang-sanyuan-jiuyun]. **Worked example.** For
2030: (2030 − 1864) / 20 = 8 remainder 6, so the ninth period, 九運, which
began in 1864 + 8 × 20 = 2024.

### 손 없는 날

The lunar days ending in 9 and 0, the 9th, 10th, 19th, 20th, 29th and 30th
of any month [wikipedia-ko-son-eomneun-nal]. **Worked example.** 7 January
2026 is the Korean lunar 11/19 of 2025, so it is one; 16 February 2026 is
12/29, and the next day, the lunar New Year, is not [superkts-son-2026].

## What is carried

In `hc-holiday`, as traditions ([`observances.md`](../observances.md)):

- `obon-july` and `obon-august`: お盆 as the span 13–16, the 迎え火 on the 13th
  and the 送り火 on the 16th, from 1873. `obon-lunar`: 旧盆 as the span
  7/13–7/15 and Okinawa's ウンケー, ナカビ and ウークイ, on the 旧暦. Three
  reckonings are three tables ([policy.md](../policy.md) §5).
- `tori-no-ichi`: 一の酉, 二の酉 and 三の酉, from 1873.
- `hatsuuma`: 初午, 二の午 and 三の午 of February, from 1873; `hatsuuma-lunar`:
  初午 of the second lunar month.
- `inoko` and `tokanya` on the 旧暦; `inoko-november` and
  `tokanya-november` on the Gregorian calendar, from 1873.

The 旧暦 is the one Japanese almanacs print: the 天保暦's rules continued at
the Japanese meridian, `hc-calendars-lunar`'s
`japanese_tenpo::UNBOUNDED_PARAMETERS`, which is the 天保暦 itself, at Kyoto,
until 1872. The lunar tables answer from 1844, when the 天保暦 took effect,
to 2146. The rule cannot number the months of 2033–34, and the Observatory
sets out three resolutions that put the tenth month of 2033 on 23 October
or on 22 November, and agree on the seventh [nao-rekiwiki-2033]; so 亥の子
and 十日夜 of 2033 are gaps, not guesses, and 旧盆 is answered. The
Observatory lists 2147–48 as the next year the rule leaves open, without
the months, which is why nothing after 2146 is answered. The Gregorian
tables start in 1873, the first Gregorian year in Japan; when each custom
moved to its Gregorian date is in no source read.

In `hc-almanac`:

- `lucky_direction`: `LuckyDirection`, the four points with their names,
  branches, azimuths and the nearest of the sixteen compass points, and
  `lucky_direction_of_year`, by the Gregorian year's number.
- `nine_periods`: `Period`, its number, era, star, years, and the ruler's
  name as the source writes it; `period_of_year`, and `period`, which turns
  at 立春 at a meridian through `nine_stars::nine_star_year`.
- `days_without_son`: `is_day_without_son`, on `dangi`, 1645 to 2150.

Not carried:

- **The places that light the 送り火 on the 15th**, keep ウークイ on the 16th,
  or keep お盆 on 1 August or on the weekend nearest the 15th; the Miyako and
  Yaeyama names of the lunar days [wikipedia-ja-obon].
- **A shrine's own day**: the 鷲宮神社's 大酉祭 on the first 酉 of December,
  川越's 熊野神社 on 3 December [wikipedia-ja-tori-no-ichi]; the 初午 of the
  箭弓稲荷神社, the first 午 of Gregorian March [yakyu-inari-hatsuuma-2026],
  of the 豊川稲荷東京別院, the first 午 after 節分 [note-hatsuuma-2026], and
  the first 午 after the lunar New Year [yasiro-hatsuuma-2026]. Each is a
  reckoning of its own that no source read dates for more than one shrine.
- **The day a year's 恵方 takes over.** `lucky_direction_of_year` takes the
  year by its number. The customs read use the Gregorian year: 恵方参り on
  New Year's Day, and the 恵方巻 of 節分, the eve of 立春, facing the year
  that has already begun [allabout-eho-2026, jre-eho-2026]. The other
  方位 deities, 大将軍, 金神 and the rest, are not carried.
- **The 大三元 of 540 years**, whose epoch the source does not give; and
  anything the source says about where the ruling star stands.
- **Where *son* is on the other days**: the source gives no table.

## Accuracy

The rules are exact given the calendars under them, and every table is
checked against dated lists:

- `tori-no-ichi` reproduces all forty-nine 酉 days of November 2009–2028 in
  Wikipedia's table, and 鷲神社's 7 and 19 November 2026.
- `hatsuuma` gives HugKum's 初午 of 2025–2029 and 伏見稲荷大社's of 2026, and
  `hatsuuma-lunar` its 21 March 2026.
- `inoko` gives KOYOMI NOTE's nine days of 2020–2028, `inoko-november` All
  About's 2 November 2025, `tokanya` 暦生活's 22 November 2023 and 日本文化研究
  ブログ's 18 November 2026.
- `obon-lunar` gives Wikipedia's 18 August 2024, 6 September 2025 and
  27 August 2026, its earliest and latest days of 1991–2030, and not the
  leap month of 2006.
- `lucky_direction` gives デジタル大辞泉's table for every year of
  1900–2099 and the directions of 2026–2030 that JRE Media and All About
  publish.
- `nine_periods` gives the source's nine periods and 立春 2024.
- `days_without_son` gives all sixty-eight days of 2026 in superkts.com's
  list, on the lunar dates it prints beside them.

The lunar dates are those of the full lunisolar calculation, not
`hc-seasons`' minimal derivation for 六曜, which differs on some months of
2033–34 ([zassetsu-and-rokuyo.md](zassetsu-and-rokuyo.md)). The dated
lists are secondary: they are computed calendars or articles, not
almanacs, and no printed almanac was read.
