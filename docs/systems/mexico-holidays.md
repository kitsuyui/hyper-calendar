# Mexico's federal days of rest and the states' own

Mexico's days of rest are federal law for every employer, and each state
adds days of its own for its public servants. This document covers
`hc-holiday`'s `MEXICO` table and its regions, the thirty-two federal
entities.

## What it is

The Ley Federal del Trabajo, article 74, lists the días de descanso
obligatorio: 1 January, the first Monday of February, the third Mondays
of March and November, 1 May, 16 September, 25 December, the federal
executive's handover every six years, and election days. It binds every
employer under Apartado A of article 123 of the Constitution, which is
every private one.

A state's own public servants are under the state's law, one per state:
a Ley del Servicio Civil, a Ley de los Trabajadores al Servicio del Estado
or the like. Each has an article on the days of rest, and some add days
of the state's own to the federal list; many instead leave the days to an
official calendar the state's government publishes each year. A day a
state law adds closes the state's and its municipalities' offices and
binds no private employer.

## How it works

Jalisco's law [mx-jal-ley-servidores] reads, in article 38, "Serán
considerados como días de descanso obligatorio" the federal days, and
among them four more: 5 May, 28 September, 12 October and 2 November. It adds the days the state's
governor sets by agreement published in the *Periódico Oficial*. So on
Monday 2 November 2026 Jalisco's state offices are closed while a private
employer in Guadalajara owes no day off: `MEXICO` asked for `MX-JAL` gives
the day as `Kind::Government`, and the day remains a business day.

## What is carried

- **The federal days** of article 74, nationwide, as the table carried
  them before this document.
- **Jalisco's four days** (`MX-JAL`), `Kind::Government`, from 2007: the
  text read is article 38 as reformed by Decreto 21593/LVII/06, published
  on 2 December 2006, and the years before it are a gap: the law is of
  1984, and its article 38 before the reform was not read. The copy
  read lists reforms to December 2009; whether a later reform changed the
  article was not seen.
- **Not yet carried, and why:** every other state. Their laws are
  published as PDF or Word files, which were not read, or leave the days
  to a yearly calendar that was not researched. The table below says what
  was searched for each. The days the governor of Jalisco adds by yearly
  agreement are not carried either.

## Accuracy

`crates/hc-holiday/tests/mexico_states.rs` checks Jalisco's four days
against article 38 as read: each in 2007 and 2026 with its kind, region
and instrument, none in 2006 and a gap there, none nationwide or in Mexico City, and
2 November 2026 still a business day. Article 38 names the four days by
their dates alone, so their names in the table are their dates; the
federal handover day it names without a date is the federal table's.

## Code

`crates/hc-holiday/src/countries/mexico.rs`: the federal rules and
Jalisco's, built by `jalisco`, in `MX_RULES`, and the table `MEXICO`. The
exchange `XMEX` includes the nationwide days only. Anchors: the tests in
`crates/hc-holiday/tests/mexico_states.rs` and, for the federal days,
`mexico_moved_three_holidays_onto_mondays_in_2006` and its neighbours in
`crates/hc-holiday/tests/countries.rs`.
