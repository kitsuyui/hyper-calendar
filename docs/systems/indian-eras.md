# The Indian eras: year counts over the Hindu months, and the Faṣlī years

Backs the identifiers `vikram-samvat-kartikadi`, `rajyabhisheka-saka`,
`saptarshi`, `magi-san`, `fasli-madras`, `fasli-bombay` and `sur-san` in
`hc-calendars-indic`, and the unregistered year arithmetic of the Gupta,
Valabhī and Kalachuri eras.

## What it is

India has dated by many eras at once. Sewell and Dikshit's *The Indian
Calendar* (1896) describes some thirty in its Art. 71 [sewell1896,
pp. 40–47]; the library already carries the Kali Yuga (`hindu-old-*`), the
Śaka (`hindu-lunar`), the Chaitrādi Vikrama (`vikrama-year`), the Bengali
San, the Kollam era, Nepal Sambat and the Odia Anka, and this document
adds the eras that are *offsets over months the library already has*:
the same tithis, the same amānta or pūrṇimānta months, or the same solar
months or civil days, with another year number that turns on another day.

The eras fall into three groups.

**Lunisolar eras over the Hindu months.**

- *The Kārttikādi Vikrama Saṃvat.* "In Northern India the year is
  Chaitradi, and its months purnimanta, but in Gujarat it is Karttikadi
  and its months are amanta" [sewell1896, p. 41]. Gujarat and its
  emigrants begin the Vikrama year at Kārttika śukla 1, the day after
  Dīpāvalī; Drik Panchang prints it as the "Gujarati Samvat", 2081 on
  21 October 2025 and 2082 from the 22nd [drik-day-panchang-2025]. Some
  parts of Kathiawar and Gujarat begin it instead at Āṣāḍha
  [sewell1896, p. 41].
- *The Rājyābhiṣeka Śaka*, the Maratha "Raja Saka", "established by
  Sivaji ... on the day of his accession to the throne, i.e., Jyeshtha
  sukla trayodasi (13th) of Saka 1596 expired, 1597 current"; "the number
  of the year changes every Jyeshtha sukla trayodasi; the years are
  current; in other respects it is the same as the Southern luni-solar
  amanta Saka years" [sewell1896, p. 47]. Wikipedia dates the coronation
  6 June 1674 and gives the same tithi [wikipedia-shivaji]; the date is
  Julian: by the library's astronomy the sunrise tithi of the Julian
  6 June 1674 is śukla 13, and that of the Gregorian 6 June, ten days
  earlier, is not.
- *The Saptarṣi-kāla or Laukika era* of Kashmir, "the only mode of
  reckoning mentioned in the Raja-Tarangini", counted from the supposed
  motion of the seven Ṛṣis (Ursa Major) through one nakṣatra in 100
  years. "In practice the hundreds are omitted"; "Kashmirian astronomers
  make the era, or at least one of its cycles of 2700 years, begin with
  Chaitra sukla 1st of Kali 27 current"; "disregarding the hundreds we
  must add 47 to the Saptarshi year to find the corresponding current
  Saka year, and 24–25 for the corresponding Christian year. The years
  are Chaitradi"; Kielhorn found them "mostly current years, and the
  months mostly purnimanta" [sewell1896, p. 41].
- *The Gupta era* of the Gupta kings and Nepal: Fleet concluded "that its
  years are current and Chaitradi; that the months are purnimanta; and
  that the epoch, i.e., the beginning of Gupta Samvat 0 current, is Saka
  242 current (A.D. 319–20)" [sewell1896, p. 43]. Wikipedia, after
  Salomon, adds 320 or 321 to a current Gupta year for the Christian one
  [wikipedia-gupta-era].
- *The Valabhī era* of Kathiawar, "merely a continuation of the Gupta era
  with its name changed", whose year "was thrown back from Chaitra sukla
  1st to the previous Karttika sukla 1st", so that its epoch "is
  synchronous with the current Karttikadi Vikrama year 376 (A.D. 318–19,
  Saka 241–42 current)"; "its months seem to be both amanta and
  purnimanta" [sewell1896, p. 43]. Wikipedia gives its later inscriptions
  as Kārttikādi and amānta [wikipedia-gupta-era].
- *The Chedi or Kalachuri era*: Kielhorn's conclusion from ten
  inscriptions of the years 793 to 934 is "that the 1st day of the 1st
  current Chedi year corresponds to Asvina sukla pratipada of Chaitradi
  Vikrama 306 current, (Saka 171 current, 5th Sept., A.D. 248); that
  consequently its years are Asvinadi; that they are used as current
  years; that its months are purnimanta" [sewell1896, p. 42–43;
  wikipedia-kalachuri-era].

**A solar era over the Bengali months.** *The Magi San* of Chittagong "is
very similar to the Bengali-san, the days and months in each being
exactly alike. The Magi is, however, 45 years behind the Bengali year,
e.g., Magi 1200 = Bengali 1245" [sewell1896, p. 45, citing Girisa
Chandra's *Chronological Tables*, not read].

**The Faṣlī years.** The *faṣl*, "harvest", year was introduced "as some
say, by Akbar, originally derived from the Muhammadan year, and bearing
the same number, but beginning in July"; "its epoch is apparently A.H. 963
(A.D. 1556), when its number coincided with that of the purely lunar
Muhammadan year, and from that date its years have been solar or
luni-solar" [sewell1896, p. 44]. The reckoning differed by region:

- *Madras*: "the Fasali year originally commenced with the 1st day of the
  solar month Adi (Karka), but about the year 1800 A.D. the British
  Government, finding that this date then coincided with July 13th, fixed
  July 13th as the permanent initial date; and in A.D. 1855 altered this
  for convenience to July 1st, the present reckoning" [sewell1896, p. 44].
  The revenue offices of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu still
  count it, July to June, the Christian year of July being the Faṣlī year
  plus 590: "Fasli year 1410 was from July 2000 – June 2001"
  [wikipedia-fasli-calendar].
- *Bombay*: "In parts of Bombay the Fasali begins when the sun enters the
  nakshatra Mrigasirsha, viz., (at present) about the 5th or 6th June"
  [sewell1896, p. 44]. Wikipedia puts the first day on 7 or 8 June
  [wikipedia-fasli-calendar, citing Pillai 1996, not read].
- *Bengal and the north-west*: a lunisolar pūrṇimānta year from Āśvina
  kṛṣṇa 1, its days numbered 1 to 30 from the full moon without the
  fortnights [sewell1896, p. 44].

"In Southern India the Fasali year 1302 began on June 5th, 1892, in
Bombay, and on July 1st, 1892, in Madras" [sewell1896, p. 44].

*The Mahratta Sūr-san* (Shahūr-san, Arabi-san) "is nine years behind the
Fasali of the Dakhan, but in other respects is just the same; thus, its
year commences when the sun enters the nakshatra Mrigasirsha", and "to
convert it to an A.D. year, add 599"; it "only diverged from the Hijra in
A.D. 1344" [sewell1896, p. 45].

**Current and expired years.** Sewell and Dikshit's tables give every
era's *current* year; the library's Śaka and Chaitrādi Vikrama years are
the *expired* ones the almanacs print today, one less. The eras here are
counted as their sources say they are written: the Kārttikādi Vikrama in
the count Drik Panchang prints (the Chaitrādi Vikrama year for Kārttika
to Phālguna), which Kielhorn found is the expired count of the
inscriptions, "the rule for those who use the Vikrama era"
[sewell1896, p. 41]; the Rājyābhiṣeka, Saptarṣi, Gupta, Valabhī and
Kalachuri years as current years, as Sewell and Dikshit give them.

## How it works

**An era over the lunisolar months** is three numbers: the month whose
tithi opens the year, that tithi, and how far the Śaka year in which a
year opens is from that year's number. Every date keeps the tithi, the
fortnight, the month and its intercalary or repeated days of the
underlying calendar — `hindu-lunar` for amānta months, or
`hindu-lunar-purnimanta` for pūrṇimānta — and only its year changes:

| Era | Opens at | Months | Year *y* opens in the Śaka year (expired) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kārttikādi Vikrama | Kārttika śukla 1 | amānta, numbered from Kārttika | *y* − 135 |
| Rājyābhiṣeka Śaka | Jyeṣṭha śukla 13 | amānta, numbered from Chaitra | *y* + 1595 |
| Saptarṣi | Chaitra śukla 1 | pūrṇimānta, from Chaitra | *y* − 3154 |
| Gupta | Chaitra śukla 1 | pūrṇimānta, from Chaitra | *y* + 241 |
| Valabhī | Kārttika śukla 1 | amānta, from Kārttika | *y* + 240 |
| Kalachuri | Āśvina śukla 1 | pūrṇimānta, from Chaitra | *y* + 169 |
| Vira Nirvana (for comparison) | Kārttika śukla 1 | amānta, from Kārttika | *y* − 605 |

A day before the opening in its Śaka year belongs to the year before: the
Rājyābhiṣeka year 351 opens at Jyeṣṭha śukla 13 of Śaka 1946 and holds
the days of Śaka 1947 up to its Jyeṣṭha śukla 12.

The offsets come from the sources' own equations. The Kārttikādi Vikrama
year for Kārttika to Phālguna "is the same as the Chaitradi year", and one
less from Chaitra to Āśvina [sewell1896, Art. 74, p. 48]: the Chaitrādi
year is the Śaka year plus 135. The Rājyābhiṣeka year 1 opened in Śaka
1596 expired. The Saptarṣi year 1 is Kali 27 current, and a current Kali
year is the current Śaka year plus 3179 (S&D's Table II heads the Śaka
year 1000 with Kali 4179 [sewell1896, Art. 103]), so the Saptarṣi year is
the current Śaka year plus 3153, the expired one plus 3154. Gupta 0 is
Śaka 242 current, so Gupta 1 opened in Śaka 242 expired. Valabhī 1 opened
five months earlier, at the Kārttika of Śaka 241 expired. Chedi 1 opened
in Āśvina of Śaka 171 current, 170 expired.

A year that opens at śukla 1 opens with the first month of its name,
the intercalary one in a year that has it, as Nepal Sambat and the Vira
Nirvana Samvat do. A year that opens in the middle of a month opens in the
ordinary month, as the Odia Anka does ([odia-anka.md](odia-anka.md)),
since the intercalary month precedes it and the source does not say that
the day moves; and where the opening tithi holds no sunrise, on the first
day after it. Neither case was found in a source.

Months are numbered from the opening month where the year opens at its
first day, as the Vira Nirvana Samvat's are, and from Chaitra where the
year opens in the middle of a month — the Rājyābhiṣeka year at Jyeṣṭha
śukla 13, or a pūrṇimānta year at śukla 1, which is the middle of the
pūrṇimānta month.

**The dropped hundreds.** A Saptarṣi date names the year within its
century: Laukika 47 is any year whose full count ends in 47. The library
counts the full year from Kali 27 current and carries the dropped form
beside it as the extra field `laukika-year`, the full year modulo 100;
the full year of a Laukika year near a given year is
`saptarshi::full_year_near`. Sewell and Dikshit's "as soon as the
reckoning reaches 100, a fresh hundred begins from 1" [sewell1896, p. 41]
does not settle whether the hundredth year is written 100 or 0; the extra
field gives 0 and `full_year_near` accepts either.

**The Magi San** is the Bengali solar calendar of `hindu-solar-bengali`,
its months and days exactly, with the year 45 less: the Gregorian year of
Boishakh less 638.

**The Faṣlī years** keep no months of their own here. Sewell and Dikshit
say that the solar Faṣlī year's "names of months, their periods of
beginning, and the serial number of days are the same as in the Hijra
year, but the year changes its numerical designation on a stated solar
day" [sewell1896, p. 44], and give no example of such a date; so the
library carries the one thing the sources pin down — the year number and
the day it changes — over the Gregorian months and days, the reckoning the
revenue records of British India printed beside it. A date is the Faṣlī
year, the Gregorian month, 1 for January, and the day. A year from the
Mṛgaśira ingress that opens on a later day of June than the one before
holds that day twice — Faṣlī 1302 opened on 5 June 1892 and 1303 on
6 June 1893, so 1302 has two 5 Junes and no 29 February — and the second
is marked as a repeated day, `leap_day` in the date's fields, as a
calendar marks a second day of the same name.

- `fasli-madras`: the year Y opens on 1 July of Y + 590. Before 1855 it
  opened on 13 July, and before about 1800 on 1 Āḍi. Sewell and Dikshit do
  not say whether 1 July first opened the year in 1855 or in 1856, so the
  calendar begins on 13 July 1855, from which Faṣlī 1265 holds under
  either reading.
- `fasli-bombay`: the year opens on the day the Sun enters Mṛgaśira, the
  fifth nakṣatra, 53°20′ of sidereal longitude, found as
  `nakshatra::solar_nakshatra_ingress_after` finds it with the Lahiri
  ayanāṃśa; the Christian year of that day less 590 is the year.
- `sur-san`: the same days, the year nine less, the Christian year of the
  ingress less 599.

**Which day the ingress opens.** Sewell and Dikshit give one day, 5 June
1892. The Lahiri ingress of 1892 falls at 23:38 UT on 5 June, 04:41 on
6 June by the local mean time of Ujjain, the meridian of their tables
[sewell1896, Art. 73]: before sunrise on the 6th, and so in the Hindu day
that began at sunrise on the 5th. The library takes that day, the rule
`SankrantiRule::SunriseDay` at Ujjain, the rule the Vikrami solar months
use; a rule by the civil day, midnight to midnight, would give 6 June and
disagree with the source. The same rule gives 7 June 2024 (ingress 19:37
UT) and 8 June 2025 (01:47 UT), the "7 or 8 June" Wikipedia gives.

**Worked example: 22 October 2025 and 15 June 2025.** On 22 October 2025
`hindu-lunar` gives Kārttika śukla 1 of Śaka 1947. Kārttika opens the
Kārttikādi year, so the year is 1947 + 135 = 2082, and Kārttika is its
month 1: 1 Kārttika 2082, Drik Panchang's "Gujarati Samvat 2082" of that
day [drik-day-panchang-2025]. The day before is amāvāsyā of Āśvina, month
12 of 2081. On 15 June 2025 the Sun has entered Mṛgaśira (on 8 June, as
above), so it is in the Bombay Faṣlī year of 2025 − 590 = 1435 and the
Sūr-san 2025 − 599 = 1426; it is before 1 July, so it is still Madras
Faṣlī 1434, which opened on 1 July 2024.

## What is carried

- **`vikram-samvat-kartikadi`**, **`rajyabhisheka-saka`** and
  **`saptarshi`**, as `LunarEra` values in `lunar_era`, over the
  *Rashtriya Panchang*'s reckoning — the Central Station's sunrise, the
  Lahiri ayanāṃśa — and so over its range, Chaitra 1700 to March 2300.
  Their era codes are `vs`, `rajyabhisheka-saka` and `saptarshi`; the
  Saptarṣi date carries the extra `laukika-year`. `LunarEra::new` builds
  one over another place's or another ayanāṃśa's calendar; `new_year`
  gives a year's first day. The Rājyābhiṣeka Śaka's use is recorded from
  6 June 1674 (Julian) with no end, since Sewell and Dikshit say it was not
  in use in 1896 and date no last day; the other two as undated.
- **The Gupta, Valabhī and Kalachuri eras** as `EraYear` values in
  `lunar_era` — `GUPTA`, `VALABHI`, `KALACHURI` — with the year of any
  amānta date and the opening day of a year, and **not registered**. The
  eras were written from the fourth century to the thirteenth — the Gupta
  and Valabhī inscriptions run from the year 82 to 945, the Chedi dates
  Kielhorn examined from 793 to 934 [sewell1896, pp. 42–43] — and the true
  lunisolar calendar here converts 1700 to 2299 only, so a registered
  calendar would convert no day anyone wrote in them, and `Usage` has no
  honest end to give it.
- **`magi-san`**, as `hindu_solar::MAGI`, the Bengali calendar under the
  era code `magi-san`.
- **`fasli-madras`**, **`fasli-bombay`** and **`sur-san`**, as
  `FasliCalendar` values in `fasli`, over the Gregorian months and days;
  the era codes `fasli` and `sur-san`. `new_year` gives a year's first
  day. The Madras calendar runs from 13 July 1855; the other two over the
  Sun's ingresses of 1700 to 2299, the range of the crate's other solar
  reckonings.
- **Not carried, and why:**
  - *The Āṣāḍhādi Vikrama year* of Kathiawar: Sewell and Dikshit say it
    begins at Āṣāḍha but not at which tithi or in which reckoning of the
    month, and give no dated example.
  - *The Bengal Faṣlī*: its days are numbered 1 to 30 from the full moon
    without the fortnights, and the source's worked example converts
    "approximately"; no dated Bengal Faṣlī day was read.
  - *The Faṣlī and Sūr-san months*, which the source calls Hijri, and
    the Madras years before 1855, whose opening at 13 July is dated only
    "about the year 1800".
  - *The Harṣa, Chālukya, Siṃha, Lakṣmaṇa Sena and Ilāhī eras* of
    Art. 71, which the roadmap did not plan.

**Known disagreements and limits.**

- *Art. 103's Chedi 828.* The text of Art. 103 pairs Āṣāḍha of Śaka 1000
  with "Chedi or Kalachuri 828"; the heading of Table II, part ii, over
  the same months, prints 829, which is what Kielhorn's epoch gives. The
  library follows the epoch.
- *One dated Bombay Faṣlī day.* The rule for which civil day the ingress
  opens rests on 5 June 1892 alone. Sewell and Dikshit reckon with the
  *Sūrya Siddhānta*, whose Sun stands hours from the Lahiri one
  ([hindu-calendars.md](hindu-calendars.md)); an ingress near sunrise may
  fall on the other day in their reckoning.
- *The Sūr-san's first day* is not dated by any source read; its days are
  the Bombay Faṣlī's by Sewell and Dikshit's "in other respects is just
  the same".
- *The Saptarṣi months* follow Kielhorn's "mostly purnimanta" of the
  inscriptions; no modern Kashmiri almanac was read to say which the
  Laukika year of today uses.
