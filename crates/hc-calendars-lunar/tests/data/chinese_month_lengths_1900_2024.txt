# Month lengths of the Chinese calendar, 1900-2024, as the Purple Mountain
# Observatory publishes them.
#
# One line per Chinese year, from the year whose first month began on
# 31 January 1900 to the one that began on 10 February 2024:
#
#     <Gregorian date of the first day of the first month>  <leap>  <lengths>
#
# <leap> is the ordinal of the month the leap month repeats (閏四月 is 4),
# 0 if none.  <lengths> has one character per month in order, 'L' for a
# 30-day month and 'S' for a 29-day one, with the leap month in its place,
# so a line with a non-zero <leap> has thirteen characters and every other
# line twelve.
#
# Provenance: 中国科学院紫金山天文台, 《1900—2025年日历》, the day-by-day
# table published on the Observatory's site (pmo-calendar-1900-2025 in
# docs/references.bib), read on 2026-09-27.  Its compilation notes say that
# the years 1900-1911 follow the 《时宪书》 published by the Qing, 1912-1948
# the 《中华民国历书》 of the former Central Observatory and the 《国民历》
# of the former Academia Sinica, and 1949-2025 the model of GB/T 33661-2017:
# so the lines are the calendar as it was promulgated, not a recomputation.
# The table was read as text, every one of its 46 021 days checked against
# the continuous sexagenary day count, and the first days of the months
# taken from the days it labels 初一 with a month name.  The last year, the
# one that began on 29 January 2025, is left out because the table ends
# before it does.
#
# The file is data for the test tests/chinese_published.rs::
# the_chinese_calendar_is_the_purple_mountain_observatorys_from_1900_to_2024,
# and docs/systems/east-asian-lunisolar.md says what it showed.
